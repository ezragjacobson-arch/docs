Safe handling is not simply a ruleset you memorise and recite — it is a set of habits that must become so automatic they require no conscious thought. Every negligent discharge in history is traceable to at least one violated safety principle. By building correct habits from your very first handling session, you make accidents structurally impossible rather than merely unlikely. The techniques covered on this page form the non-negotiable baseline for every responsible firearm owner.

## The Four Safety Rules (Expanded)

These rules apply to every firearm, every time, without exception. Expand each rule to understand the reasoning behind it and the common ways it gets violated.

<Accordion title="Rule 1: Treat Every Firearm as if It Is Loaded">
  **The principle:** You must handle every firearm — regardless of whether you know it to be unloaded — with the same care you would apply to a loaded one. The moment you allow yourself to relax this assumption, you have created the conditions for a negligent discharge.

  **Why it matters:** Mechanical failures, lapses in memory, and handoffs between people all introduce uncertainty about a firearm's status. A firearm that was clear ten minutes ago may not be clear now. Treat the hardware as inherently dangerous at all times.

  **Common violations:**
  - Pointing a "just-cleared" firearm at people or yourself
  - Dry-firing carelessly in a room with live ammunition present
  - Passing a firearm to another person without first verifying it is clear
  - Assuming a firearm handed to you by someone else is unloaded
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Rule 2: Never Point at Anything You Are Not Willing to Destroy">
  **The principle:** At all times, maintain conscious control over where your muzzle is directed. The muzzle must never sweep across people, animals, or property you are not prepared to shoot.

  **Why it matters:** This rule operates as a backstop against Rule 1. Even if a firearm were to discharge unexpectedly, a correctly directed muzzle means no one is harmed. The combination of Rules 1 and 2 creates two independent barriers against tragedy.

  **Common violations:**
  - Holstering or unholstering while the muzzle sweeps the support hand or leg
  - Flagging bystanders while scanning or moving with a drawn firearm
  - Resting a muzzle pointed toward your own body while seated or waiting
  - Casual handling at home with the muzzle drifting toward other people in the room
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Rule 3: Finger Off the Trigger Until on Target and Ready to Fire">
  **The principle:** Your trigger finger must remain straight and outside the trigger guard at all times — indexed high on the frame above the trigger — until your sights are on an intended target and you have consciously decided to fire.

  **Why it matters:** The trigger is the mechanical interface between intention and action. Placing your finger inside the trigger guard before you are ready to fire removes a critical delay between stimulus and discharge. Under stress, startle responses and grip tightening can cause inadvertent trigger movement.

  **Common violations:**
  - Placing the finger on the trigger immediately upon gripping the firearm
  - Finger slipping inside the guard during the drawstroke before the muzzle is on target
  - Re-engaging the trigger guard when moving between targets or after a threat has passed
  - Demonstrating a firearm to someone and instinctively wrapping the finger around the trigger
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Rule 4: Know Your Target and What Lies Beyond It">
  **The principle:** Before pressing the trigger, positively identify your target and understand what is behind it, beside it, and between you and it. You are legally and morally responsible for every round you fire and every surface it strikes.

  **Why it matters:** Bullets do not stop at the target. They can over-penetrate, ricochet, or miss entirely. In a home defence scenario, family members may be in adjacent rooms. In an outdoor environment, a missed shot can travel hundreds of metres. Target identification also prevents tragic misidentifications.

  **Common violations:**
  - Firing at movement or a silhouette without positive identification
  - Failing to account for backstop when practising dry-fire at home
  - Using inappropriate ammunition that over-penetrates walls in a home defence context
  - Engaging a threat in a crowded or indoor environment without considering collateral risk
</Accordion>

## Handling a Firearm Safely

Whenever you pick up, receive, or prepare to put down a firearm, run through this standard clearing procedure. Make it a ritual you never skip.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Point in a Safe Direction">
    Before doing anything else, orient the muzzle toward a safe direction — a direction in which an unintended discharge would cause no injury or minimal property damage. A berm, a solid exterior wall, or the floor at an angle may all qualify depending on your environment.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Remove the Magazine (If Applicable)">
    For semi-automatic pistols and rifles, press the magazine release and remove the magazine from the firearm. Set it aside where you can see it. Note that removing the magazine does not unload the chamber — a round may still be present.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Lock the Slide or Bolt Open">
    Retract the slide fully (pistol) or cycle and lock the bolt open (rifle/shotgun) to expose the chamber. On many semi-automatic pistols, lock the slide back using the slide stop lever while the slide is rearward.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Visually and Physically Inspect the Chamber">
    Look directly into the chamber with adequate lighting. Tilt the firearm if necessary to catch the light. Then insert a finger (keep it out of the trigger guard) and physically verify the chamber is empty. Do not skip the physical check — low-light conditions can make a chambered round hard to see.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm Clear Before Handling Further">
    Only after both visual and physical inspection confirm an empty chamber should you proceed with whatever task is next — cleaning, inspection, dry-fire practice, or storage. If you are handing the firearm to another person, perform this check in front of them and invite them to verify for themselves.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Loading and Unloading

Correct loading and unloading technique varies by action type. Select your platform below.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Semi-Automatic Pistol">
    **Loading:**
    1. Point the firearm in a safe direction and confirm the chamber is clear before loading.
    2. Load your magazine to the desired round count — avoid over-compressing the spring by loading to the manufacturer's rated capacity.
    3. Insert the magazine firmly into the magazine well until it clicks and seats.
    4. Rack the slide fully rearward and release it completely to chamber the first round. Do not ride the slide forward.
    5. Perform a press-check if desired: slightly retract the slide to visually confirm a round is chambered.
    6. Engage any external safety if present.

    **Unloading:**
    1. Point in a safe direction. Keep your finger outside the trigger guard.
    2. Press the magazine release and remove the magazine.
    3. Rack the slide fully rearward to eject the chambered round.
    4. Lock the slide open and visually and physically inspect the chamber.
    5. Retrieve and account for the ejected round.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Revolver">
    **Loading:**
    1. Point in a safe direction. Keep your finger outside the trigger guard.
    2. Open the cylinder by pressing the cylinder release latch and pushing the cylinder out to the side.
    3. Visually confirm all chambers are empty.
    4. Load rounds into individual chambers or use a speed loader or moon clip for faster loading.
    5. Close the cylinder firmly until it latches.

    **Unloading:**
    1. Point in a safe direction.
    2. Open the cylinder and rotate the muzzle upward.
    3. Depress the ejector rod firmly and fully to push all cases and unfired rounds clear.
    4. Visually inspect each chamber to confirm all are empty.
    5. Close the cylinder only after confirming clear.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Semi-Auto Rifle">
    **Loading:**
    1. Point in a safe direction. Engage the manual safety.
    2. Insert a loaded magazine and ensure it locks in place.
    3. Retract the charging handle fully and release it to chamber the first round. Do not ease it forward — let it fly.
    4. Confirm the safety is in the desired position before proceeding.

    **Unloading:**
    1. Point in a safe direction. Engage the safety.
    2. Press the magazine release and remove the magazine.
    3. Pull the charging handle fully rearward to eject the chambered round, then lock the bolt open if the bolt catch allows.
    4. Visually and physically inspect the chamber.
    5. Account for the ejected round.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Pump Shotgun">
    **Loading:**
    1. Point in a safe direction. Engage the safety.
    2. Keep the action open. Load shells into the magazine tube through the loading port, base-first, until the tube is full or desired round count is reached.
    3. To chamber a round, close the action by releasing the action lock (if applicable) and pumping the fore-end fully forward.
    4. Top off the magazine tube if loading to full capacity.

    **Unloading:**
    1. Point in a safe direction. Engage the safety.
    2. Open the action by pressing the action release and cycling the fore-end rearward to eject the chambered shell.
    3. Press the shell lifter down, reach into the loading port, and depress the shell stop to allow shells to feed out of the magazine tube one at a time while continuing to cycle the action. Repeat until no more shells feed.
    4. Visually and physically inspect the chamber and magazine to confirm empty.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## The Drawstroke (Handgun)

Drawing from a holster is a high-risk moment that benefits enormously from structured technique and deliberate practice. A rushed or incorrect drawstroke is a common cause of accidental discharges and self-inflicted injuries. Learn this sequence with an unloaded firearm before ever practising with live ammunition.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Establish Your Grip">
    Drive your strong-side hand to the firearm while it is still in the holster and obtain your full firing grip — high on the backstrap, thumb high, fingers wrapped. You should not need to adjust your grip after the draw. Establish it in the holster.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Clear the Holster">
    Pull the firearm straight up, clearing the top of the holster completely before moving the muzzle in any other direction. Disengage any retention devices before or during this motion as appropriate to your holster design.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rotate the Muzzle Onto Target">
    From the high-compressed position near your chest, rotate the muzzle forward and down until it is oriented toward the target. At this point you have a retention shooting position if extreme close range demands it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Join Your Support Hand">
    Bring your support hand to meet the strong hand at the centreline of your body. Establish your full two-handed firing grip before extending. Never sweep your support hand with the muzzle.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Extend to the Target">
    Push the firearm out toward the target along the line of sight, bringing your sights into alignment with the target. Your finger enters the trigger guard only at this point, when the muzzle is on target and you have confirmed your decision to fire.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Trigger Discipline

Your trigger finger is the final mechanical link in the chain of events that produces a discharge. Managing it with absolute discipline is the single most important physical habit you will develop as a firearm owner.

Keep your trigger finger indexed — straight and pressed flat against the frame above the trigger guard — at all times when you are not actively firing. This applies during the draw, during movement, during reloading, and during any scan for additional threats after an engagement. The finger does not enter the trigger guard until your sights are on an identified target and your mind has made the deliberate decision to fire. That sequence is non-negotiable.

<Warning>
  Trigger discipline failures kill and injure people every year. The startle response, sympathetic squeeze (involuntary grip tightening when the body is startled or tenses), and poor technique under adrenaline are all known causes of unintended discharges. The only reliable defence against all of them is having your finger outside the trigger guard — not on the side of the frame, not resting lightly on the guard, but straight and indexed above it — until the moment of intentional fire. Practise this until it is a reflex.
</Warning>

## Muzzle Discipline

Where your muzzle points at any given moment is your responsibility and yours alone. In a home environment especially, the layout of rooms means the muzzle of a drawn or carried firearm can sweep walls behind which family members are sleeping, sitting, or moving. Develop a constant awareness of muzzle orientation throughout every handling session and every moment of carry.

When moving through your home with a firearm, use a low-ready or high-compressed ready position rather than extending the firearm toward doors and walls. When sitting, be conscious of where the muzzle of a holstered or resting firearm is oriented. When setting a firearm down, place it with the muzzle directed toward a safe backstop. This awareness becomes effortless with practice — but it must be deliberately built.

## Range Etiquette

Shooting ranges operate on shared safety agreements. Respecting these rules protects every person on the line.

- **Always obey range officer commands immediately and without question.** A range officer calling "cease fire" requires you to stop shooting, take your finger off the trigger, and wait for further instruction.
- **Never cross the firing line while anyone is downrange.** The firing line is the boundary between the shooting position and the target area. No one moves forward of it until a cease fire is called, all firearms are unloaded and benched, and the range officer authorises the movement.
- **Treat all firearms as loaded at all times**, even between strings of fire and even when a firearm is benched.
- **Uncase and handle firearms only at the firing line or in designated safe areas.** Never walk through a range with a firearm out of its case except in designated handling zones.
- **Immediately report any unsafe behaviour** to the range officer — do not attempt to correct another shooter yourself unless you are an instructor or range safety officer.
- **Clean up after yourself.** Collect brass where required, dispose of target materials correctly, and leave the bench in better condition than you found it.
