Handguns are the most popular choice for personal defence due to their compact size and ease of carry. Whether you are protecting your home or carrying concealed, understanding the fundamental differences between handgun types — and how calibre choices affect performance — helps you select the right tool for your specific situation and skill level.

## Semi-Automatic Pistols vs. Revolvers

Both platform types have proven track records in self-defence. Your choice comes down to how each aligns with your training, hand strength, and intended use.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Semi-Automatic Pistols">
    Semi-automatic pistols feed cartridges from a detachable magazine housed in the grip. When you fire, the expanding gas drives the slide rearward, ejecting the spent case, cocking the hammer or striker, and chambering the next round from the magazine.

    **Advantages**
    - Higher round capacity (typically 10–17+ rounds in standard magazines)
    - Faster reloads by swapping magazines
    - Flatter profile for easier concealed carry
    - Wide variety of aftermarket accessories and holsters

    **Disadvantages**
    - More mechanical components mean more potential failure points
    - Susceptible to limp-wristing malfunctions (the shooter's weak grip prevents the slide from cycling fully)
    - Requires more training to clear malfunctions correctly

    **Popular Examples**
    - Glock 17 / Glock 19 (9mm)
    - SIG Sauer P320 (9mm)
    - Smith & Wesson M&P9 (9mm)
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Revolvers">
    Revolvers store cartridges in a rotating cylinder. Pulling the trigger (double-action) or manually cocking the hammer (single-action) rotates the cylinder and aligns the next chamber with the barrel before firing.

    **Advantages**
    - Mechanically simple with fewer failure modes
    - Will fire even if a round fails to ignite — just pull the trigger again
    - No magazine to lose, damage, or fail
    - Easier for shooters with limited hand strength to operate

    **Disadvantages**
    - Lower capacity (typically 5–6 rounds)
    - Slower reloads, even with a speed loader or moon clips
    - Bulkier cylinder makes concealed carry more challenging

    **Popular Examples**
    - Smith & Wesson Model 686 (.357 Magnum)
    - Ruger GP100 (.357 Magnum)
    - Smith & Wesson Model 642 (.38 Special, snub-nose)
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Common Calibres

Calibre selection affects recoil, capacity, terminal performance, and ammunition availability. Study each option carefully before deciding.

<Accordion title="9mm Luger (9x19mm)">
  The 9mm is the world's most popular defensive handgun calibre. Modern hollow-point projectiles have closed the performance gap with larger calibres significantly. You benefit from higher magazine capacity, lower recoil for faster follow-up shots, and widely available, affordable ammunition for practice. The FBI and most US law enforcement agencies use 9mm as their standard issue.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title=".40 S&W">
  Developed in the early 1990s to offer performance between the 9mm and .45 ACP, the .40 S&W delivers a heavier projectile at higher velocity than 9mm. You get slightly more energy on target but also notably more felt recoil and muzzle flip. Many law enforcement agencies have transitioned away from .40 S&W back to 9mm in recent years, though it remains a capable defensive option.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title=".45 ACP">
  The .45 ACP fires a large-diameter, relatively slow-moving bullet. It has a long history in American military and law enforcement service. You trade capacity and lower recoil for a wide projectile that creates a large wound channel. Magazine capacity is typically 7–10 rounds in full-size pistols. The .45 ACP is well-suited to shooters who prefer fewer, heavier rounds.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title=".38 Special / .357 Magnum">
  These two calibres share the same bullet diameter (.357 inches) and are the dominant revolver cartridges for defensive use. .38 Special offers manageable recoil and is the standard loading for snub-nose revolvers. .357 Magnum is a lengthened, higher-pressure version delivering significantly more velocity and energy — but also considerably more recoil and muzzle blast. A revolver chambered in .357 Magnum will also accept .38 Special ammunition, giving you flexibility during training and practice.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title=".380 ACP">
  The .380 ACP (also called 9mm Short or 9x17mm) is the calibre of choice for compact and subcompact pistols intended for deep concealment. Recoil is light and the guns are small enough to carry in a pocket holster. The trade-off is reduced energy compared to full-size 9mm loads. Use high-quality hollow-point ammunition to maximise terminal performance from this smaller cartridge.
</Accordion>

## Choosing Your Handgun

No single handgun is the best choice for every person or situation. Work through these considerations before you buy.

**Intended use** — A home-defence pistol can be larger and heavier because you will not carry it all day. A concealed-carry firearm must balance shootability with the ability to disappear under everyday clothing. Some shooters own one of each.

**Hand size and grip** — A pistol that does not fit your hand properly forces poor trigger mechanics and weakens your grip. When you visit a dealer, handle several models with both hands before committing. Many manufacturers offer interchangeable backstraps to adjust grip circumference.

**Action type** — Double-action/single-action (DA/SA) pistols have a long, heavy first trigger pull followed by shorter, lighter subsequent pulls. Striker-fired pistols (like the Glock) offer a consistent pull on every shot. Single-action-only (SAO) pistols require the hammer to be cocked before each shot. Choose the trigger type that you will train with consistently.

**Budget** — Factor in the cost of the firearm, at least two spare magazines, a quality holster, a cleaning kit, a safe, and a substantial quantity of practice ammunition. A reliable defensive pistol from a reputable manufacturer typically starts around $400–$600 USD.

<Tip>
  Visit a range that offers a rental programme before you buy. Shooting a pistol for 50 rounds tells you far more about fit and feel than simply handling it at a counter. Many ranges stock a wide variety of models specifically for this purpose.
</Tip>

## Essential Accessories

Once you select your handgun, equip yourself with these foundational accessories before your first range visit.

- **Holster** — Choose a holster appropriate to your carry method (IWB, OWB, or bedside). Ensure it fully covers the trigger guard and retains the firearm securely.
- **Spare magazines** — Carry or store at least two additional magazines beyond the one in the pistol.
- **Hearing protection** — Both foam earplugs and over-ear muffs are acceptable; doubling up (earplugs plus muffs) is recommended for indoor ranges.
- **Eye protection** — Ballistic-rated glasses or safety glasses protect against ejected brass and carbon particles.
- **Cleaning kit** — A basic pistol cleaning kit with a bore brush, patches, cleaning rod, and CLP is sufficient to start.
- **Safe storage** — A locking handgun safe or biometric vault keeps your firearm secure and inaccessible to unauthorised users, especially children.
