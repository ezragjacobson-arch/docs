A clean firearm is a reliable firearm. Carbon fouling, unburned powder, and metal residue accumulate with every round you fire, and over time they degrade accuracy, cause malfunctions, and accelerate wear on critical components. Regular maintenance prevents these problems, extends the service life of your handgun, and is an inseparable part of responsible ownership. Make cleaning a habit — not an afterthought.

## Tools and Supplies You'll Need

Gather everything before you begin so you are not handling a partially disassembled firearm while searching for equipment.

- Cleaning rod (segmented or one-piece, calibre-matched)
- Bore brush (bronze or nylon, in your pistol's calibre)
- Patch holder / jag
- Cleaning patches (cotton, in the correct size for your calibre)
- Nylon utility brush (an old toothbrush works well)
- Microfibre cloths or lint-free rags
- CLP (Cleaner, Lubricant, Protectant) or dedicated bore cleaner
- Gun oil or dedicated lubricant (e.g., Slip 2000 EWL, Lucas Gun Oil)
- Solvent (e.g., Hoppe's No. 9 or Ballistol)
- Toothbrush or dedicated parts brush for detail cleaning
- Cleaning mat or old towels to protect your work surface

## Safety First

<Warning>
  Treat every firearm as if it is loaded until you have personally confirmed otherwise. Before any cleaning session, complete all of the following steps in order — do not skip or reorder them.

  1. Point the muzzle in a safe direction (toward a wall with no one on the other side, or into a clearing barrel if available).
  2. Remove the magazine. Set it aside away from the firearm.
  3. Lock the slide (or bolt) to the rear to expose the chamber.
  4. Visually inspect the chamber — look for the absence of brass.
  5. Physically inspect the chamber — insert a finger to confirm it is empty.
  6. Only after completing steps 1–5 should you begin disassembly.

  Never clean a loaded firearm. Never allow live ammunition on the same surface where you are cleaning.
</Warning>

## Disassembly

Field-stripping breaks your handgun down into its major subassemblies for routine cleaning. You do not need to fully disassemble the trigger group for regular maintenance.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Semi-Automatic Pistol">
    Field-strip procedures vary by manufacturer, but most modern striker-fired and hammer-fired pistols follow a similar sequence. Consult your owner's manual for your specific model.

    **General Field Strip Sequence**
    1. Confirm the pistol is unloaded (see Safety First above).
    2. Lock the slide to the rear using the slide stop.
    3. Remove the magazine if not already done.
    4. Locate the takedown mechanism — this may be a lever (Glock, SIG), a button (Beretta), or a rotating disassembly latch (HK). Engage it per your manual.
    5. Release the slide stop while holding the slide, then push the slide forward off the frame rails.
    6. Remove the recoil spring assembly from the underside of the slide.
    7. Tilt the barrel at the rear and lift it out of the slide.
    8. You now have four components: frame, slide, recoil spring assembly, and barrel.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Revolver">
    Revolvers do not require field-stripping in the same way as semi-automatics. Most routine cleaning is done with the cylinder open.

    **General Revolver Cleaning Sequence**
    1. Open the cylinder by pressing the cylinder release latch and swinging the cylinder out to the left (for most double-action revolvers).
    2. Use an ejector rod to push out any spent cases.
    3. Inspect each chamber individually — visually and physically confirm all are empty.
    4. The cylinder can be cleaned in the open position on most revolvers; no further disassembly is needed for routine maintenance.
    5. For deeper cleaning, consult your owner's manual — many revolvers allow the cylinder to be removed by pulling the ejector rod forward, but this varies significantly by model.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Cleaning the Bore

The bore accumulates the most significant fouling and requires the most attention. Work from the chamber end toward the muzzle whenever possible to push fouling out the front.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Apply Solvent to the Bore Brush">
    Saturate your calibre-specific bronze bore brush with bore solvent or CLP. Do not apply solvent directly into the bore — apply it to the brush.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scrub the Bore">
    Push the bore brush through the barrel from the chamber end to the muzzle. Make 8–10 complete passes (each pass is one push through and one pull back). The bronze bristles scrub away copper jacket fouling and carbon deposits from the rifling.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run Solvent-Soaked Patches">
    Replace the brush with your patch holder and jag. Run a solvent-soaked patch through the bore and discard it. Repeat with fresh patches until each patch comes out visibly cleaner than the last.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Dry Patch">
    Run a completely dry, clean patch through the bore to remove any remaining solvent residue. The patch should come out white or very lightly grey.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply a Light Oil Patch">
    Run a patch with a single drop of gun oil through the bore. This leaves a thin protective film that prevents rust without attracting excessive fouling during storage. Do not over-oil — excess oil in the bore can cause a dangerous pressure spike if the bore is obstructed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Cleaning the Frame and Slide

With the pistol field-stripped, use a nylon brush or toothbrush dampened with solvent to scrub the following areas:

**Frame**
- The slide rails — carbon and grit accumulate here and increase friction
- The feed ramp — a dirty feed ramp is a common cause of failures to feed
- The magazine well — wipe it out with a cloth

**Slide**
- The interior of the slide, especially the breech face (the recessed area where the cartridge head sits)
- The extractor claw and the area around it
- The ejection port

After scrubbing, wipe every surface dry with a clean microfibre cloth. Ensure no solvent residue remains before lubricating — many solvents are slightly corrosive and should not be left on metal surfaces.

## Lubrication

<Tip>
  With firearms, less lubrication is almost always better than more. Over-lubrication attracts dirt and powder fouling, turns into a thick paste that impedes movement, and can migrate into the firing mechanism. Apply oil sparingly to the following points only.

  **Key Lubrication Points (Semi-Automatic Pistol)**
  - **Slide rails** — one small drop of oil on each rail (two on the frame, two on the slide)
  - **Barrel hood and barrel feet** — a thin wipe where the barrel contacts the slide
  - **Recoil spring assembly** — a light wipe along the spring and guide rod
  - **Barrel exterior** — a thin protective coat before reassembly

  Apply oil with a clean cloth, fingertip, or dedicated applicator — never pour or squirt oil directly onto internal components. After applying, cycle the action several times to distribute the lubricant before reassembling.

  For revolvers, apply a small amount of oil to the crane (the arm the cylinder swings out on), the ejector rod, and the exterior of the barrel. Apply a drop to each side of the hammer pivot.
</Tip>

## Reassembly and Function Check

<Steps>
  <Step title="Reassemble the Barrel into the Slide">
    For semi-autos: tilt the barrel into position in the slide, seating the hood at the rear.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Seat the Recoil Spring">
    Place the recoil spring assembly under the barrel with the correct end forward (consult your manual — orientation matters on many designs).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reattach the Slide to the Frame">
    Align the slide with the frame rails and push the slide rearward onto the frame. You will feel the slide seat onto the frame rails and drop into place.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Engage the Takedown Mechanism">
    Return the takedown lever, pin, or latch to its locked position.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Perform a Function Check">
    With the pistol confirmed unloaded: rack the slide several times to confirm smooth movement. Press the trigger — confirm the striker or hammer falls. Rack the slide again, engage the safety (if present) and verify the trigger does not function. Disengage the safety and confirm the trigger resets correctly. Insert an empty magazine and confirm it seats and releases cleanly.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Maintenance Schedule

| Interval | Tasks |
|---|---|
| **After every range session** | Clean the bore, wipe fouling from the slide and frame rails, re-lubricate lightly, perform function check |
| **Monthly (or after 250+ rounds)** | Full field strip and clean as described above; inspect recoil spring for deformation or wear; check all pins and screws for tightness |
| **Annually** | Professional inspection by a qualified gunsmith; check barrel throat for wear; replace recoil spring if not done in the past year; inspect extractor and ejector tension; test reliability with carry ammunition |
