Firearms are among the most effective self-defence tools available to responsible adults, but they demand far more than simple ownership. To use a firearm safely and legally, you must commit to rigorous training, disciplined safe handling, and a thorough understanding of the laws that govern purchase, possession, and carry in your jurisdiction. This section gives you the foundational knowledge to begin that journey with confidence.

## Choosing the Right Firearm

No single firearm is right for every person or every situation. Before making a purchase, work through the following considerations carefully.

**Purpose** is your starting point. A firearm intended for home defence does not need to be concealable, so you can prioritise capacity and ease of use over size. If you intend to carry concealed on your person, weight, dimensions, and holster compatibility become critical. For outdoor or wilderness use, you may need a platform that handles weather, longer distances, or larger threats.

**Ergonomics** matter more than most beginners expect. A firearm that does not fit your hand, that you cannot operate the controls on confidently, or that produces recoil you struggle to manage will undermine your ability to use it effectively under stress. Always handle a firearm before purchasing, and ideally shoot it first.

**Calibre** is the ongoing debate in the self-defence community. Common defensive calibres include 9mm, .40 S&W, and .45 ACP for handguns, and .223/5.56 or .308/7.62 for rifles. Modern defensive ammunition has narrowed the performance gap between calibres considerably. Choose a calibre you can shoot accurately and comfortably, and that your chosen firearm is chambered for reliably.

**Budget** should cover more than the firearm itself. Factor in holsters, ammunition for training, cleaning supplies, a quality storage solution, and the cost of formal instruction. Cutting corners on any of these categories increases risk.

<Tip>
  Rent before you buy. Most shooting ranges offer rental programmes that let you fire several different models before committing to a purchase. Spending an hour with a rental can save you hundreds of dollars and ensure you choose a firearm you will actually enjoy training with.
</Tip>

## The Four Safety Rules

Every credible firearms training programme is built on four non-negotiable safety rules. Memorise them. Internalise them. Apply them every single time you handle a firearm.

<Note>
  **The Four Universal Firearm Safety Rules**

  1. **Treat every firearm as if it is loaded** — even if you just unloaded it yourself, even if someone else tells you it is empty.
  2. **Never point a firearm at anything you are not willing to destroy** — keep the muzzle directed away from people, animals, and property you do not intend to engage.
  3. **Keep your finger off the trigger until your sights are on target and you have made the decision to fire** — your trigger finger lives straight and outside the trigger guard at all times until that moment.
  4. **Know your target and what lies beyond it** — bullets can penetrate, miss, or ricochet; you are legally and morally responsible for every round you fire.

  Violating any single rule is dangerous. Violating two simultaneously is how negligent discharges injure or kill people.
</Note>

## Legal Considerations

<Warning>
  Firearm ownership, purchase, storage, and carry laws vary significantly between countries, states, and even municipalities. What is legal in one jurisdiction may be a serious criminal offence in another. Before purchasing any firearm, research the specific laws that apply where you live, work, and intend to carry. Obtain every required licence, permit, and registration before taking possession. Consult a qualified legal professional if you are uncertain. Guardian Defence does not provide legal advice, and nothing on this site should be interpreted as such.
</Warning>

## Getting Started

If you are new to firearm ownership, follow these steps in order. Skipping steps — particularly the legal and training steps — exposes you and others to unnecessary risk.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Research Local Laws and Licencing Requirements">
    Identify the specific legislation that governs firearm ownership, storage, and carry in your country, state or province, and municipality. Determine what licences, permits, or background checks are required before you can legally purchase or possess a firearm.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Take a Certified Firearms Safety Course">
    Enrol in and complete a recognised firearms safety course before handling or purchasing a firearm. Courses from organisations such as the NRA, USCCA, or your national equivalent provide foundational knowledge and hands-on practice under qualified instruction.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a Firearm Appropriate to Your Needs">
    Using the guidance in the Choosing the Right Firearm section above, select a platform, calibre, and specific model that matches your purpose, fits your hands, and suits your budget. Rent and test options at a local range before purchasing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Store and Transport Legally and Safely">
    Before bringing your firearm home, ensure you have a compliant storage solution in place. Review the [Storage and Transport](/firearms/storage-and-transport) guide and understand your jurisdiction's transport requirements.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Practice Regularly at a Qualified Range">
    Competence under stress requires consistent, deliberate practice. Schedule regular range sessions, seek ongoing instruction, and consider scenario-based training as your skills develop. A firearm you cannot operate reliably is not a self-defence asset.
  </Step>
</Steps>
