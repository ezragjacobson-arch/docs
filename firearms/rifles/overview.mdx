Rifles offer superior accuracy, range, and stopping power compared to handguns, making them effective choices for home defence and outdoor use. A rifle's longer barrel produces higher projectile velocity, its longer sight radius improves accuracy, and the two-hand, shoulder-mounted platform gives you far more control under recoil. However, their size demands different storage, handling, and situational awareness — particularly in confined spaces. Understanding which platform and calibre match your needs is the essential first step.

## Rifle Types

<Accordion title="AR-Platform (Semi-Automatic)">
  The AR-platform — originally designed by ArmaLite, most famously produced by Colt — is the most popular rifle design in the United States. "AR" stands for ArmaLite Rifle, not "assault rifle." The platform's modular design allows you to swap upper receivers, stocks, handguards, and accessories to configure the rifle precisely for your use case.

  **How it works:** Gas from a fired cartridge travels rearward through a tube or piston system, cycling the bolt carrier group to extract the spent case and chamber a fresh round from the detachable box magazine.

  **Common calibres:** .223 Remington / 5.56x45mm NATO (interchangeable in most chambers), .300 Blackout (optimised for suppressed use and short barrels), 6.5 Grendel, 6.8 SPC.

  **Advantages:** Modular and highly customisable, low recoil, standard magazine capacity of 20–30 rounds, enormous ecosystem of accessories and spare parts, straightforward maintenance.

  **Popular examples:** Colt LE6920, Daniel Defense DDM4, BCM RECCE-16, Springfield Armory SAINT.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Bolt-Action Rifle">
  Bolt-action rifles require the shooter to manually operate a handle to extract the spent case and chamber a new round after each shot. This deliberate single-shot cycle limits rate of fire but simplifies the mechanism and contributes to exceptional inherent accuracy.

  **How it works:** Lifting and pulling the bolt handle rearward unlocks the bolt from the receiver, extracts and ejects the spent case, and cocks the firing pin. Pushing the bolt forward chambers the next round from the internal or detachable magazine; rotating the handle down locks the bolt.

  **Common calibres:** .308 Winchester, .30-06 Springfield, 6.5 Creedmoor, .243 Winchester, .338 Lapua Magnum for long range.

  **Advantages:** Mechanically simple and extremely reliable, feeds virtually any cartridge shape, inherently accurate, easy to clean, legal in virtually all jurisdictions.

  **Popular examples:** Remington Model 700, Ruger American, Tikka T3x, Savage Axis.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Lever-Action Rifle">
  Lever-action rifles use a lever integral to the trigger guard to cycle the action. They are one of the oldest repeating rifle designs, with roots in the American frontier era. Modern lever-actions remain accurate, smooth, and capable defensive and hunting tools.

  **How it works:** Sweeping the lever forward and back extracts the spent case, cocks the hammer, and chambers a fresh round from a tubular or box magazine.

  **Common calibres:** .30-30 Winchester (most common hunting lever calibre), .357 Magnum, .44 Magnum, .45 Colt, .450 Marlin.

  **Advantages:** Smooth, fast action; typically legal in jurisdictions that restrict semi-automatic rifles or magazine capacity; pairs nicely with revolvers that share the same calibre; evocative, reliable design with a proven track record.

  **Popular examples:** Winchester Model 94, Marlin 336, Henry Big Boy.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="AK-Platform (Semi-Automatic)">
  The AK-platform, originally designed by Mikhail Kalashnikov as the AK-47, is one of the most widely produced and recognised rifle designs in the world. Semi-automatic variants are legally available in the US and many other countries.

  **How it works:** Long-stroke gas piston system — the piston is attached to the bolt carrier and travels the full length of the receiver with each shot. This design is notably tolerant of dirt, debris, and lack of lubrication.

  **Common calibres:** 7.62x39mm (the original calibre, delivers significant energy at moderate distances), 5.45x39mm (Russian military standard), .223/5.56 (AK-platform rifles chambered for NATO calibres exist).

  **Advantages:** Legendary reliability even in adverse conditions, robust and simple construction, widely available ammunition.

  **Popular examples:** WASR-10, PSA AK-47 GF3, Zastava ZPAP M70.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title=".22 LR Rifles (Training and Small Game)">
  .22 LR rifles are not primary defensive tools, but they are among the most valuable firearms you can own for training. Their minimal recoil, low cost, and near-zero noise (especially suppressed) make them ideal for building fundamentals.

  **How it works:** Available in semi-automatic, bolt-action, and lever-action configurations. The rimfire cartridge is ignited by the firing pin striking the rim of the case rather than a centre primer.

  **Common platforms:** Ruger 10/22, Marlin Model 60, CZ 455/457, Smith & Wesson M&P15-22 (AR-platform .22 LR for training).

  **Advantages:** Extremely low recoil, affordable ammunition for high-volume practice, wide availability, effective for small game and pest control.
</Accordion>

## Common Calibres

<Accordion title=".223 Remington / 5.56x45mm NATO">
  These two cartridges are nearly identical in dimensions but differ in pressure specifications. 5.56 NATO is loaded to higher pressure than .223 Rem. A chamber marked 5.56 NATO will safely fire both; a chamber marked .223 Rem should only fire .223 Rem ammunition. This is the dominant AR-platform cartridge and offers low recoil, high velocity, flat trajectory, and over-penetration characteristics that make it surprisingly suitable for home defence when loaded with frangible or hollow-point ammunition.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title=".308 Winchester / 7.62x51mm NATO">
  A full-power rifle cartridge used extensively in hunting, precision shooting, and military applications. The .308 delivers significantly more energy than .223/5.56, making it effective at distances well beyond 500 yards in capable hands. Recoil is substantial compared to intermediate calibres. Over-penetration through walls is a serious concern for home-defence use.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title=".300 Blackout (300 BLK)">
  Designed to maximise performance from short AR-platform barrels, the .300 Blackout uses the same case head as 5.56 NATO (meaning you can use the same lower receiver and magazine) but fires a heavier .30-calibre projectile. It was engineered for suppressed use — subsonic loads run quietly through a suppressor while supersonic loads maintain meaningful terminal performance at moderate distances.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="7.62x39mm">
  The original AK-47 cartridge. It fires a relatively heavy bullet at moderate velocity, producing energy similar to the .30-30 Winchester. At distances under 300 yards, it is a capable cartridge for both hunting and defensive use. It hits harder at close range than 5.56 NATO but has a significantly arching trajectory beyond 200 yards.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title=".22 LR">
  The world's most popular cartridge by volume. The .22 LR is not a defensive cartridge in most contexts, but it is unmatched for training due to its negligible recoil and cost. Many AR-platform rifles have .22 LR conversion kits available, allowing you to train with the same manual of arms as your defensive rifle at a fraction of the cost.
</Accordion>

## Rifles for Home Defence

When you consider a rifle for home defence, several factors shift compared to hunting or range use.

**Barrel length and manoeuvrability** — A standard 16-inch AR-pattern rifle is over 32 inches long even with the stock collapsed. In the hallways and rooms of a typical home, this requires deliberate technique to avoid flagging walls and corners. Shorter-barrelled rifles (SBRs) offer better manoeuvrability but are regulated under the NFA in the United States (see Legal Considerations). A pistol-calibre carbine is another option worth considering.

**Over-penetration** — Rifle rounds — particularly .308 and 7.62x39 — penetrate building materials aggressively. For home defence, consider .223/5.56 with quality frangible or hollow-point ammunition, which tends to break up upon hitting hard surfaces rather than continuing through multiple walls.

**Suppressor use** — Where legally permitted, a suppressor on a home-defence rifle significantly reduces the risk of permanent hearing damage when firing indoors without ear protection, and reduces disorientation from muzzle blast. Suppressors do not make rifles "whisper quiet" as depicted in film.

**Lighting** — A weapon-mounted light is essential on any home-defence long gun. You must identify your target and the space beyond it before firing.

## Legal Considerations

<Warning>
  Firearms laws vary dramatically between countries, states, and even municipalities. Before purchasing any rifle, verify the following in your specific jurisdiction.

  - **NFA regulations (United States):** Rifles with barrels shorter than 16 inches are classified as Short-Barrelled Rifles (SBRs) and require a $200 tax stamp, a Form 4 or Form 1 application, and a waiting period. Suppressors are also NFA items. Violations carry serious federal felony penalties.
  - **Prohibited features laws:** Several US states (California, New York, Maryland, etc.) restrict or prohibit specific rifle features including pistol grips, folding stocks, flash suppressors, and threaded barrels. Know your state's specific definitions before purchasing.
  - **Magazine capacity restrictions:** Many US states and countries outside the US restrict magazine capacity to 10 rounds or fewer. Some jurisdictions ban detachable magazines entirely.
  - **Import restrictions:** Certain rifles and configurations are affected by import bans and sporting-use requirements under US federal law.

  This documentation is for educational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Always consult a qualified attorney familiar with firearms law in your jurisdiction.
</Warning>

## Choosing a Rifle

<Steps>
  <Step title="Define Your Purpose">
    Be specific. Home defence, hunting, long-range target shooting, and competition shooting each favour different platforms and calibres. A rifle optimised for one task may be poorly suited for another.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Research Legal Restrictions in Your Area">
    Before you select a platform or features list, confirm what is legal where you live. A rifle that is standard in one state may be heavily restricted or entirely prohibited in another.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose Your Calibre Based on Use Case">
    Match the calibre to the task. For home defence, prioritise manageable recoil and appropriate terminal performance. For hunting, match the cartridge to the game. For long-range shooting, prioritise ballistic coefficient and availability of match-grade ammunition.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Handle Several Options at a Dealer">
    Length of pull, weight, and ergonomics vary significantly between manufacturers and models. Handle each rifle with an unloaded chamber, mount it to your shoulder, acquire a sight picture, and operate the controls before deciding.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Take a Rifle-Specific Safety and Handling Course">
    A handgun course does not substitute for dedicated rifle training. Enrol in a rifle-specific fundamentals course from a certified instructor before your first range session with a new platform. Many organisations (NRA, USCCA, Appleseed Project) offer entry-level rifle courses nationwide.
  </Step>
</Steps>
