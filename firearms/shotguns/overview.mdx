The shotgun is arguably the most effective close-range defensive firearm available to civilians. A single trigger pull delivers a payload of multiple projectiles at high velocity, producing stopping power that handguns and most rifles cannot match at typical indoor distances. Whether you are selecting your first home-defence firearm or evaluating what you already own, understanding the key variables — action type, gauge, and ammunition — will help you make an informed decision.

## Shotgun Types

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Pump-Action">
    The pump-action shotgun is manually operated: after each shot, you cycle the forend rearward and then forward to eject the spent shell and chamber a fresh round. This manual operation makes the pump-action extraordinarily reliable — it will cycle virtually any shell regardless of pressure level or condition, and it has far fewer moving parts to fail than a self-loading design.

    **Advantages:**
    - Highest mechanical reliability of any shotgun action
    - Lower purchase price than comparable semi-automatics
    - Spare parts and accessories are widely available
    - Audible racking sound has documented deterrent value

    **Common Models:**
    - Mossberg 500 / 590 — affordable, proven, ambidextrous safety
    - Remington 870 — decades of law enforcement use, excellent aftermarket support

    **Defensive Use:** The pump-action is the go-to recommendation for most home-defence scenarios. Its reliability under adverse conditions and forgiving ammunition tolerance make it an outstanding choice for a firearm that may sit in a safe for years before being needed.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Semi-Automatic">
    Semi-automatic shotguns use either a gas-operated or inertia-driven system to automatically cycle the action after each shot. This means you can fire as fast as you can pull the trigger without manually working the forend, giving you faster follow-up shots and reduced felt recoil compared to a pump of the same gauge.

    **Advantages:**
    - Faster follow-up shots with no manual cycling
    - Reduced perceived recoil (especially gas-operated designs)
    - Less shooter fatigue during extended range sessions
    - Modern designs are highly reliable with quality ammunition

    **Common Models:**
    - Benelli M2 / M4 — inertia-driven; exceptional reliability; widely used by military and law enforcement
    - Mossberg 930 — gas-operated; affordable; popular home-defence option

    **Cost Considerations:** Quality semi-automatic shotguns command a significant price premium over pump-actions. Expect to pay $700–$1,800 or more for a reliable defensive semi-auto. They are also more ammunition-sensitive — some designs cycle light loads poorly, so confirm your chosen firearm functions reliably with your chosen defensive load.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Break-Action / Double-Barrel">
    Break-action shotguns — whether side-by-side or over-under — tip open at a hinge in front of the receiver to allow loading and unloading. They are the simplest possible shotgun action with virtually no mechanical components to fail.

    **Considerations for Defence:**
    - Limited to two rounds before a full reload is required
    - Extremely simple operation requires minimal training
    - No magazine tube to extend; capacity cannot be increased
    - Excellent as a rural utility firearm; adequate for basic home defence

    The break-action is not the primary recommendation for dedicated home defence due to its capacity limitation, but it is far better than nothing, and two well-placed rounds of 00 buckshot are highly effective. If a double-barrel is what you have, learn to reload it quickly under stress.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Gauge Selection

<Accordion title="12 Gauge">
  The 12 gauge is the universal standard for defensive shotgunning. It offers the widest selection of ammunition types, the most aftermarket support, and the highest terminal performance of any commonly available gauge. Every defensive ammunition manufacturer produces 12-gauge loads, and you will find it on the shelf of every sporting goods store worldwide.

  - **Recoil:** The highest of the three options listed here, though manageable with proper technique and reduced-recoil loads
  - **Ammunition selection:** Broadest available — buckshot, slugs, reduced-recoil, frangible, and specialty loads
  - **Recommendation:** The first choice for any shooter who can comfortably manage the recoil

  If you are unsure which gauge to buy, buy a 12 gauge.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="20 Gauge">
  The 20-gauge fires a smaller shell and produces noticeably less recoil than the 12 gauge — roughly 30–35% less felt recoil depending on load. This makes it an excellent choice for smaller-framed shooters, younger family members, or anyone with a shoulder injury or sensitivity to recoil.

  - **Recoil:** Significantly reduced compared to 12 gauge
  - **Terminal performance:** Still highly effective at defensive distances; a standard 20-gauge load of #3 buckshot produces devastating wound ballistics inside 15 yards
  - **Ammunition selection:** Narrower than 12 gauge but still widely available in defensive configurations
  - **Recommendation:** A legitimate defensive choice — do not let anyone convince you the 20 gauge is inadequate for home defence
</Accordion>

<Accordion title=".410 Bore">
  The .410 is the smallest common shotgun bore. It fires a very light payload and produces minimal recoil, making it appealing for recoil-sensitive shooters. However, its defensive utility is limited.

  - **Recoil:** Lowest of any shotgun gauge
  - **Terminal performance:** Significantly lower than 12 or 20 gauge; most .410 defensive loads produce fewer pellets and lower energy than the alternatives
  - **Ammunition selection:** Narrower, with fewer dedicated defensive loads available
  - **Recommendation:** Not recommended as a primary home-defence choice. If the .410 is the only gauge a shooter can comfortably handle, it is better than no firearm — but actively work toward building the strength and technique to step up to a 20 gauge
</Accordion>

## Ammunition Types for Defence

<Accordion title="00 Buckshot">
  Double-aught (00) buckshot is the most widely used defensive shotgun load and the benchmark against which all others are measured. A standard 12-gauge 00 buckshot shell contains 8–9 pellets, each approximately .33 calibre — roughly the diameter of a 9mm handgun bullet. At close range, all pellets strike nearly simultaneously, creating a devastating wound channel.

  - **Best for:** Primary home-defence load in most scenarios
  - **Penetration:** High; can penetrate interior drywall walls; consider your home's layout and who may be behind walls
  - **Pattern at 15 yards:** Typically 6–10 inches depending on choke and barrel length
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="#4 Buckshot">
  Number 4 buckshot fires a larger number of smaller pellets (approximately 21–27 pellets per shell depending on load). Each individual pellet is smaller than a 00 buck pellet, reducing penetration through intermediate barriers while still delivering significant terminal effect on a human threat.

  - **Best for:** Shooters concerned about over-penetration through walls in an apartment or densely occupied home
  - **Terminal performance:** Excellent at close range; effectiveness degrades faster at distance than 00 buck
  - **Pattern:** Wider than 00 buck at equivalent distances
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Reduced Recoil Loads">
  Reduced-recoil defensive loads use a modified powder charge to drive the payload at lower velocity, cutting felt recoil substantially — often by 30–40% — while maintaining effective terminal performance at defensive distances. These are not "light" or underpowered loads; they are purpose-engineered for defensive use.

  - **Best for:** Shooters managing recoil sensitivity, younger or smaller shooters, and anyone who wants to run a full magazine of defensive rounds at the range without fatigue
  - **Performance:** Equivalent to standard loads at distances inside 25 yards
  - **Recommendation:** Strongly consider reduced-recoil 00 buckshot as your standard home-defence load; the terminal performance trade-off is minimal and the control benefits are real
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Slugs">
  A slug is a single, solid projectile — essentially a large bullet fired from a shotgun. A 12-gauge slug delivers rifle-like accuracy and enormous energy transfer, and can be fired through most cylinder-bore or improved-cylinder barrels without risking damage.

  - **Best for:** Longer-range defensive scenarios (property defence beyond 25 yards), shooting through vehicle doors or other barriers, situations where precision matters more than pattern spread
  - **Penetration:** Very high; will defeat most common cover and barrier materials
  - **Accuracy:** With rifle sights or a red dot, 3–4 inch groups at 50–75 yards are achievable
  - **Note:** Consider keeping a mix of buckshot and slugs accessible; some defensive educators recommend loading buckshot first with a slug or two as follow-up rounds
</Accordion>

## Shotgun Features to Look For

When selecting or configuring a defensive shotgun, prioritise these features:

- **Extended magazine tube** — increases capacity from the standard 4–5 rounds to 7–8 rounds
- **Ghost ring or aperture sights** — faster and more precise than a standard bead sight at longer ranges
- **Synthetic stock** — impervious to moisture, temperature, and rough handling; will not warp like wood
- **Sling attachment points** — a sling keeps your hands free while retaining the firearm
- **Tactical light rail** — a weapon-mounted light allows you to identify targets in a dark home without sacrificing your grip
- **Side saddle shell carrier** — mounts extra rounds directly on the receiver for rapid reloading

## Legal Considerations

<Warning>
  Shotgun ownership and configuration is subject to federal, state, and local law. Key restrictions include:

  - **Barrel length:** In the United States, a shotgun barrel must be at least **18 inches** in length. Cutting or possessing a barrel shorter than 18 inches without the appropriate NFA tax stamp constitutes a federal felony.
  - **Overall length:** The overall length of a shotgun must be at least **26 inches**.
  - **Short-Barrelled Shotgun (SBS):** Any shotgun with a barrel under 18 inches or overall length under 26 inches is classified as an NFA item requiring a $200 tax stamp, a background check, and approval from the ATF. Processing times vary.
  - **Suppressors:** Shotgun suppressors exist and are legal in many states with NFA approval, but they are bulky and rarely practical for home defence.
  - **State and local law:** Many states impose additional restrictions on magazine capacity, feature configuration, or the types of ammunition you may possess. Always verify the laws in your jurisdiction before purchasing, modifying, or storing a shotgun.
</Warning>
